Herbs were never taken alone

Honey is the carrier. Barley is the antidote. Ginger travels with cress. Every one of these is a real, recorded pairing — the difference is that ours arrives with the ratio written on it.

Buna & Halwo

The remedy for a running stomach, and the reason honey appears in so much Ethiopian medicine: pounded roasted coffee is taken with honey for diarrhoea and stomach upset, and across the ethnobotanical record honey is the standard carrier for root, bark and seed preparations. Green coffee, not the dark roast — less astringent, less aggressive on an empty stomach.

For
Running stomach, loose stools, the morning after heavy food
Contains
Green arabica coffee, wild highland honey, timat (Lepidium sativum) seed
How
One sachet in warm water, 30 min before breakfast. Twice on a bad day.
Caffeine

Supply

Taste
Bitter, then honey. Genuinely unpleasant, genuinely effective.
Not for
Ulcer flare, uncontrolled reflux, or before 14:00

Zinjible & Feto

For an acidic stomach. The ginger rhizome is chewed in Ethiopia for stomachache and respiratory trouble, and it is masticated together with feto seed for stomach disorders — a pairing recorded repeatedly across districts. Ginger acts as a carminative, relieving gas, and is anti-nausea. Take it before the meal, not after.

For
Acid, burning after eating, bloating, nausea before meals
Contains
Ginger rhizome (6 mg gingerols), feto seed, green cardamom
How
Half a level teaspoon in warm water, 20 min before food. Twice daily.
Caffeine
None
Taste
Sharp, hot, peppery. Fades fast.
Not for
Blood thinners, gallstones, or pregnancy without advice

Tenadam & Halwo

For stomach pain specifically. The juice of crushed Ruta chalepensis leaves is mixed with water or honey and taken orally — this is one of the clearest single-plant instructions in the ethnobotanical record. Tenadam is the herb on the saucer at Addis coffee houses, stirred into the cup. Strong, resinous, aromatic.

For
Sharp stomach pain, cramping, the coffee-house bitters
Contains
Tenadam leaf extract (aqueous), wild honey
How
One sachet in warm water. Once daily, short courses of 7–10 days.
Taste
Piney, resinous, warm. Not a pleasant drink.
Note
Hedonistically it is a bitters — take it as a digestive, not a medicine
Not for
Pregnancy — uterotonic. Children, or anyone on blood pressure medication

Dokuma & Abalo

The lab-tested one. Syzygium guineense leaf has been validated in vitro against gastrointestinal pathogens with inhibition zones of 10–26 mm and a minimum inhibitory concentration of 1.56 mg/mL against Staphylococcus aureus — the strongest antibacterial result of any plant in this library. Paired with abalo fruit powder, the specific recorded preparation for dysentery: powder with honey, fermented seven days, taken until it clears.

For
Dysentery, intestinal upset, after-travel gut problems
Contains
Dokuma leaf (80% methanol extract equivalent), abalo fruit powder, honey
How
One sachet in warm water, twice daily for 5 days only.
Evidence
In vitro. Not a clinical trial, not an antibiotic
Taste
Very bitter, very dark. Drink it anyway.
Not for
Ongoing use. See a doctor for real dysentery

Teff & Shiferaw

The foundation. Teff carries 12.8–20.99% protein, 8% fibre, iron, calcium, zinc, magnesium and B vitamins, with a glycemic index of 57–74 and no gluten — and its calcium bioavailability is reported as comparable to milk. Moringa leaf tested in Ethiopia at 28–29% crude protein and 826–1530 mg calcium per 100 g, and in animal work it prevented the cholesterol, triglyceride and LDL rise caused by a high-fat diet.

For
Everyday energy, iron, blood sugar, the 4pm slump
Contains
Whole teff, Moringa oleifera leaf, sesame (tahini), moringa flower
How
2 scoops in water, into shakshuka, or stirred into kolo
Delivers
Iron, calcium, ~7 g fibre, 12%+ protein
Taste
Nutty, faintly sweet, sesame at the end
Gluten
Free. Teff is naturally gluten-free and lacks antinutrients
Not for
Sesame allergy. Leaf only — never root or bark

Kolo & Shiferaw

Gesho & Besso

Gesho is the Ethiopian bittering plant — it gives tej and tella their bitterness, and it inhibits bacterial growth as it ferments. The leaf is used for stomachache, diarrhoea, fever, joint pain and loss of appetite, and its nutrient profile is genuinely useful: 70.5% carbohydrate, 25.6% fibre, 8.5% protein. Besso — roasted barley powder — is the recorded antidote taken alongside strong preparations, to stabilise the stomach.

For
Appetite, heaviness after meals, bloating, the 30-minute-before-food dose
Contains
Gesho leaf & stem (15%), roasted barley (besso), honey
How
Half a level teaspoon in warm water, 30 min before meals
Taste
Sharply bitter, then roasty. Bitter is the point.
Evidence
Anti-biofilm activity up to 99.9% against S. aureus, B. subtilis and S. mutans in vitro
Not for
Pregnancy; high doses are laxative and purgative

Masos & Arit

Two of the highest-use plants in the ethnobotanical record, in one spoon. Vernonia amygdalina carries 90 recorded therapeutic uses — its leaves are boiled and drunk with a cup of coffee for intestinal worms, malaria and fever. Artemisia afra is used for cough, asthma, headache, malaria, ague and stomachache. Bitter, aromatic, and not remotely a pleasant drink.

For
Fever and malaria support, cough, worms, general bitterness
Contains
Masos leaf, artemisia leaf, bisana bark, water
How
One sachet in warm water, once daily with food
Taste
Intensely bitter, sage-like, warming
Note
Supporting comfort, not a treatment for malaria — see a clinic
Not for
Pregnancy (artemisia); barks are wild-harvested

Straight answer

Half of these are traditions, not treatments.

Some of what is above has been validated in a lab — dokuma against gastrointestinal bacteria, moringa against lipids, gesho against biofilm. Most of it is recorded practice: what people have done for generations, documented carefully but not clinically tested. We think that distinction is worth printing on the lid, so we do.
