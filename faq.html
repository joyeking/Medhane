Read this before you buy

Every one of these plants is real, documented and in regular use. Several of them are also genuinely unsafe in specific situations. This page is the part most supplement sites leave out.

!

Not medical advice

Medhane products are foods and dietary supplements. Nothing here diagnoses, treats, cures or prevents any disease, and nothing replaces a clinician.

The short list

Four things on this site are not for pregnancy.

Tenadam and artemisia are uterotonic. Moringa leaf reduced fertility by 73.3% in mice at extract doses. Gesho and kosso are purgative at dose. We put this on the front of the page instead of in a footnote.

Ginger

Blood thinners

Ginger has well-documented antiplatelet activity. If you take warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel or any anticoagulant, talk to your prescriber before taking Zinjible & Feto, and do not start it the week before a procedure.

Tikur azmud

Blood sugar

Black seed can lower blood glucose. If you take metformin or insulin, do not add Shiferaw & Tikur Azmud without telling your clinician, and monitor more closely than usual in the first fortnight.

Buna

Caffeine

45 mg per sachet, roughly half a filter coffee. Not for people with uncontrolled hypertension, anxiety, arrhythmia, or anyone pregnant. We cap it at two sachets a day and advise nothing after 14:00.

Gesho

Laxation

Gesho is used as a laxative and purgative in Ethiopian practice, and it stimulates bile flow. Not for anyone with obstruction, and not alongside other laxatives. Short courses only.

Moringa

Leaf only

We sell M. oleifera leaf powder. Never root, bark or seed oil — those are used in traditional practice and are not the same as a food supplement. Moringa can also affect thyroid function and blood sugar.

Dokuma & Abalo

Not an antibiotic

The antibacterial result for Syzygium guineense was measured in a petri dish against specific bacteria. It is not a clinical result. Real dysentery, and anything with blood in the stool or a fever, needs a clinic and possibly antibiotics.

Questions

Everything else people ask

Does coffee with honey actually stop diarrhoea?

Both parts are in the traditional record. Pounded roasted Coffea arabica seed is documented for curing diarrhoea when mixed with honey, and across Ethiopian ethnobotanical studies honey is the standard carrier for root, bark and seed preparations — remedies are taken with water, skimmed milk, honey, injera or boiled coffee. Coffee also adds fluid and caffeine-free tannins at our green-coffee dose.

This is a food tradition, not a clinical treatment. Persistent diarrhoea, blood in the stool, or dehydration needs a clinic.

Why does ginger help with acid, and when does it make it worse?

In Ethiopian practice the ginger rhizome is chewed for stomachache and respiratory trouble, and masticated with feto (Lepidium sativum) seed for stomach disorders. Ginger is carminative — it relieves gas — and has documented gastroprotective effects.

It is not universal. Ginger can relax the lower oesophageal sphincter, which makes reflux worse in some people. If your symptom is burning that comes back up after eating rather than cramping and heaviness, start with a small dose and stop if it is not helping. See a doctor for recurring heartburn.

What does "in vitro" actually mean, and why is it on your labels?

In vitro means tested in a dish, a tube or an animal, not in people. It is real evidence about the chemistry, and it is not evidence that you will feel better.

For example, Syzygium guineense leaf extract inhibited Staphylococcus aureus with a minimum inhibitory concentration of 1.56 mg/ml — a genuinely strong laboratory result. Moringa leaf powder alleviated the cholesterol, triglyceride and LDL elevation caused by a high-fat diet in mice. Moringa leaf also reduced fertility by 73.3% in mice, which is why it is flagged for pregnancy.

We separate the two on every product page, because collapsing them into a single claim is how supplement companies mislead people.

How much is one serving, and can I take it while pregnant?

One pouch is 28 days. Doses are per day of use, not per kilo sold, and they are printed on the label. Children get less — the traditional practice in the literature is a quarter of an adult's coffee cup, which is roughly 2–5 ml against up to 250 ml for an adult.

Four of our nine blends are unsuitable in pregnancy. Tenadam and artemisia are uterotonic, moringa leaf reduced fertility in animal work, and fennel seed is traditionally avoided. Talk to your clinician before taking any herbal product during pregnancy, including the ones that feel gentle.

Where does the moringa actually come from?

Two species matter in Ethiopia. Moringa oleifera is widely cultivated and has been screened specifically as a locally available nutritional supplement — 28.28% crude protein, and in animal work it was more effective than M. stenopetala at preventing the lipid rise from a high-fat diet.

Moringa stenopetala is the southern Ethiopian tree — shiferaw, halako, shelagta depending on the region — cooked with kurkufa and eaten as a vegetable by around five million people. It has higher phenolics and flavonoids than M. oleifera, but the literature cautions that it needs further safety testing before use in supplements. We blend both leaf powders and label which is which.

Is there heavy metal in this stuff?

Possibly, if nobody tests it. Dried Moringa oleifera leaf powder sold in Ethiopian markets has been found at 352.0 mg/L lead in one study, and mineral content varies widely with soil, fertiliser, industrialisation and storage.

Every lot we buy goes out for lead, arsenic, cadmium and mercury, plus microbiology and marker content. The result is printed on the lid with a batch number we keep for three years. If a batch fails, it does not ship.

Do the herbs actually get harvested sustainably?

This is the part the industry skips. Ethnobotanical studies in Ethiopia consistently identify agricultural expansion, overharvesting and firewood collection as the main threats, and root harvest is by far the most destructive — roots are around 30% of recorded preparations, and bark harvest can kill the tree outright.

We buy leaf first and root only from managed stock. Where a plant is under pressure — bisana, kosso, tenadam — we work with the gatherers on harvest windows and rotation rather than buying by weight. Some plants on our library page are on that watch list and are labelled as such.

Can I take two blends at once?

Yes, but not two strong bitters together, and not without counting the caffeine. Buna & Halwo and Gesho & Besso on the same morning is 45 mg of caffeine plus a purgative-leaning bitter. The ritual we suggest instead is one bitter before food, one mineral blend with food, and the coffee-and-honey cup in the morning.

If you are on any regular medication, tell your prescriber the full list of what you are taking — including the herbs. That conversation is worth more than any label.

Are your testimonials real?

The three on the homepage are illustrative examples of the kind of feedback we get at the counter, written to show how each product is actually taken. They are not verified customer reviews, and we do not present them as such. When we have verified reviews we will label them separately.

What if it doesn't work for me?

Open pouches get a refund. That is why we sell in pouches rather than sealed jars — we would rather eat the difference than sell you four months of something you don't want. Refill pouches are also 15% off and the jar can be returned by post for credit.
