Plant catalogue

☕ Seed · roasted

Buna ቡና

Coffee arabica L.

For diarrhoea and stomach upset. Pounded roasted seed mixed with honey is a recorded remedy. Also the backbone of the coffee ceremony — a roasted seed and a bee. Contains caffeine and chlorogenic acids.

DigestionTea & coffee Caffeine
Part
Roasted seed, pounded
Carrier
Honey, or boiled coffee (buna)
Caution
Caffeine. Not after mid-afternoon
🫚 Rhizome

Zinjible ጅንጅብል

Zingiber officinale Roscoe

For an acidic stomach. The rhizome is popularly chewed in Ethiopia for stomachache and respiratory trouble, and masticated with feto for stomach disorders. Carminative — it relieves gas — and anti-nausea.

AcidRespiratory Whole rhizome
Part
Rhizome (root), fresh or dried
Pairs with
Feto seed — chewed together
Caution
Blood thinners; reflux can worsen
🌱 Leaf powder

Shiferaw ሽፈራው

Moringa stenopetala · also M. oleifera

For iron, energy and hunger. Cooked with kurkufa in the south; drunk as syrup for tiredness. Leaf tested for supplements at 28–29% crude protein, 826–1530 mg calcium and 794–904 mg potassium per 100 g.

NutritionIron Leaf only
Part
Leaf — dried and sieved
Also
M. oleifera screened better on lipids
Caution
Never root, bark or seed oil internally
🌿 Leaf & stem

Gesho ጌሾ

Rhamnus prinoides L'Hér. · shiny-leaf buckthorn

For appetite and heaviness. The Ethiopian bittering plant for tej and tella — used for stomachache, diarrhoea, fever, joint pain and appetite. Leaf extract showed anti-biofilm activity up to 99.9% against three bacteria.

BittersDigestion Grows near coffee
Part
Leaf and stem, sun-dried and pounded
Nutrition
70.5% carb · 25.6% fibre · 8.5% protein
Caution
Laxative and purgative in high doses
🌾 Seed

Feto ፌቶ

Lepidium sativum L. · garden cress

Masticated with ginger for stomach disorders. Also given for internal worms in several recorded preparations, usually pounded with other herbs and taken with honey. Sharp, peppery, fast-acting.

DigestionSeed
Part
Seed
Pairs with

Caution

🌿 Leaf juice

Tenadam ጤናዳም

Ruta chalepensis · "Adam's Head"

For stomach pain. The juice of crushed leaves is mixed with water or honey and taken orally. Sits on the saucer at Addis coffee houses and gets stirred into the cup. Strong, aromatic, resinous.

DigestionPotent
Part
Leaf, crushed to juice
Carrier
Water or honey
Caution
Not for pregnancy — uterotonic
🌾 Leaf & root

Arit አሪት

Artemisia afra

For cough, cold and fever. Used for coughs and asthma, and recorded for headache, malaria, ague, fever, stomachache and as a diuretic. One of the most phytochemically profiled Ethiopian plants.

RespiratoryFever
Part
Leaf; root for fever and smoke
Form
Dried leaf, infused
Caution
Not in pregnancy; neurotoxic in high dose
⚫ Seed

Tikur Azmud ትኩር አዝሙድ

Nigella sativa L. · black seed

The most-studied seed in the country. Profiled for phenolics, tannins, alkaloids, saponins, terpenoids, flavonoids and reducing sugars. Crushed and taken with honey; also stirred into coffee.

DigestionWell studied
Part
Seed, crushed
Carrier
Honey, or added to buna
Caution
May affect blood sugar and clotting
🍃 Leaf · very bitter

Masos ማሶስ

Vernonia amygdalina Delile

For malaria, worms and the gut. Carries 90 recorded therapeutic uses in ETM-DB — among the highest of any Ethiopian plant. Leaves are pounded, boiled and drunk with a cup of coffee for intestinal worms.

Bisana ቢሳና

Croton macrostachyus Hochst. ex Delile

The most-used plant in the database. 128 recorded therapeutic uses — the highest figure of any Ethiopian species. Leaf decoction for malaria; root bark with butter for internal complaints.

Hagenia abyssinica (Bruce) J.F.Gmel.

For worms and stomach complaints. One of the highest fidelity-level plants in Ethiopian ethnobotany — 93.75% consistency of reported use, alongside Zehneria scabra at 95%.

For influenza and allergies. Used across Ethiopian sites for influenza and allergic complaints. Steam-inhaled and drunk as a bitter infusion. The name means "the smell of the sea" — the highlands' eucalyptus belts.

Brucea antidysenterica J.F.Mill.

For dysentery, fever and weight loss. Fruit powder mixed with honey and fermented for seven days, taken orally until it clears. One of the longest and most specific preparation instructions in the literature.

Ximenia americana L.

For wounds, stomachache and tonsillitis. Oil from the fruit kernel is applied to fresh wounds to prevent infection. The fruit is eaten, the root used internally. Grows in acacia woodland and along dry riverbeds.

Teff is the reason this country doesn't need a supplement aisle.

Teff is 12.8–20.99% protein, 8% fibre, high in iron, calcium, zinc, magnesium, thiamine, riboflavin, niacin, pyridoxine and folate. It is gluten-free, free of antinutrients, and its glycemic index sits at 57–74. Its calcium bioavailability is reported as comparable to milk.
